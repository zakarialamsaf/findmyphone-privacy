Privacy Policy — Find My Phone

Last updated: 25 September 2026
Contact: findmyphone.support@gmail.com

What this app does

Find My Phone lets phones that have been deliberately paired with each other ask one another to report their location, ring, or share where they are for a while.

What we collect

  • Location. Precise or approximate location. It is collected in three situations, all of which you, or somebody you have paired with, start deliberately:
    • A locate request. A paired phone asks where yours is; your phone answers once, at that moment, and stops.
    • Location sharing. You choose to share your position for a period you pick — fifteen minutes, an hour, eight hours, or until you stop it. While a session is running your phone reports its position about every thirty seconds. Sending an SOS starts a session of this kind that runs until you cancel it.
    • Stolen mode. If you have turned on “Let trusted phones report this phone stolen” in Settings, a phone you have paired with can start a sharing session on your phone remotely, so it can be found if it is taken. This is off unless you turn it on, and you can turn it off at any time. While it runs, Android shows a permanent notification saying the app is using your location; that notification cannot be hidden.

Outside these, the app does not collect your location and does not build a location history. Only the most recent position is kept — there is no trail of where a phone has been.

  • Battery level. While a sharing session is running, your battery percentage is sent with your position, so whoever is watching knows how long the phone is likely to last.
  • A device identifier. An anonymous account id issued by Firebase Authentication and a Firebase Cloud Messaging registration token, used to deliver requests to your phone.
  • A device name. Whatever you type in settings. Choose a nickname if you prefer.
  • A pairing code. A random six-character code that identifies your phone during pairing.
  • Advertising identifier. The app shows adverts served by Google AdMob, which uses your device’s advertising ID to select and measure them.

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, or contacts. The app does not request access to your address book or your messages.

The microphone and camera are never used by this app. Saying a phone’s name to find it hands off to Android’s own speech screen, which returns only text; scanning a pairing QR code uses Google Play Services’ scanner. Neither gives this app access to the microphone or the camera, and neither permission appears in its listing.

How it is used

Your location is used solely to answer a request from a phone you have paired with, or to feed a sharing session you or they started. It is never used for advertising and is never sold.

The advertising ID is used only by Google AdMob to serve adverts. AdMob does not receive your location.

Who it is shared with

  • The devices you pair with. A paired device can request your location, ring your phone, and see a sharing session you have started. Pairing is mutual and either side can end it at any time. You choose which paired phones receive an SOS in Settings.
  • Google Firebase, which hosts the authentication, database and messaging this app runs on, as our data processor. See firebase.google.com/support/privacy.
  • Google AdMob, for the adverts, which receives your advertising ID but not your location. See support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543.
  • OpenStreetMap, whose tile servers receive the map area you are viewing in order to serve the map images.

Nobody else.

How long it is kept

  • Your device record and pairings are kept until you delete them or uninstall the app.
  • The most recent shared position is kept until a newer one replaces it, or until you delete your data. Older positions are overwritten, not stored.
  • A locate answer is kept in the request record; delete it from the app, or it is removed with your device record.

Your control

  • See who can find you: Settings → Paired phones.
  • Revoke: tap any paired phone to unpair. It takes effect immediately.
  • Choose who gets an SOS: Settings → Who gets my SOS.
  • Refuse remote stolen mode: Settings → Let trusted phones report this phone stolen. It is off unless you turn it on, and turning it off stops any session it started.
  • Stop sharing at any time: the home screen shows a Stop button whenever a session is running, and a permanent notification does the same.
  • Delete everything: Settings → Delete my data. This removes your device record, your pairings and your last shared location from our service, and deletes the anonymous account itself. You can also email the address above and we will delete your data within 30 days.
  • Turn it off: revoke the location permission in Android settings, or uninstall.
  • Adverts: Settings → Ad privacy choices reopens the consent form where that applies, and Android’s own settings let you reset or delete your advertising ID at any time.

Transparency

Your phone shows a notification every time a paired device locates it, and a permanent notification for as long as any sharing session is running. Both are deliberate and neither can be disabled from inside the app.

Children

This app is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit. Database rules are default-deny: a device can read only its own record, and a request from another phone is rejected by the server unless the two phones are already paired with each other.

Changes

If this policy changes materially we will update the date above and note the change in the app’s release notes.
